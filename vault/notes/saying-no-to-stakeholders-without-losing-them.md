# Saying no to stakeholders without losing them

Engineering leads spend a surprising amount of time saying no. Done badly, it burns trust. Done well, it builds it.

I never say a plain no. I say what we would have to stop doing to say yes, and I let the stakeholder weigh it with me. Most of the time they choose the existing priority themselves, and they leave the meeting feeling heard rather than blocked.

The second habit is to write it down. A two-line message after the meeting, 'we agreed to keep X ahead of Y until the end of the quarter', prevents the same conversation from happening every week.

Finally, give a real date for when you'll revisit. 'Not now' with a date is a plan. 'Not now' without one is a brush-off, and people can tell the difference.
