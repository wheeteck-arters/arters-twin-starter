---
title: Hiring for curiosity
date: 2026-04-10
---

Skills go out of date. Curiosity doesn't. When I hire engineers, I look hard for evidence that someone keeps learning without being told to.

In interviews I ask about the last thing they learned that wasn't required for their job, and how they learned it. I ask them to explain a system they've worked on to someone non-technical. And I ask what they would do in their first week if they joined a team with a codebase they'd never seen.

Strong answers are specific. They name a book, a side project, a bug they chased for days because they wanted to understand it. Weak answers are general: I like learning new things.

Curious engineers ask better questions in design reviews, debug faster and adapt to new tools quickly. In a year where AI tools change every month, curiosity has become the skill that predicts all the others.
