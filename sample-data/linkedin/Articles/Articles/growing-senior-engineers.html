How I grow senior engineers

Created on 2026-05-28

Promotion to senior is not a reward for writing more code. It's recognition that someone makes the people around them better.

When I coach engineers towards senior, I look for three shifts. First, from solving the problem in front of them to asking whether it's the right problem. Second, from owning their code to owning an outcome, including the boring parts like monitoring and documentation. Third, from being helped to helping: reviewing well, unblocking others, and explaining decisions in writing.

The fastest way I know to build these habits is to give someone a small, real project with a fuzzy edge: a migration nobody owns, a flaky service, a process that annoys everyone. Then I stay close but don't steer. The fuzzy edge is where judgement grows.

I also ask every senior on my team to write one short internal post a month about something they learned. Writing forces clarity, and it scales their influence beyond the meetings they attend.
